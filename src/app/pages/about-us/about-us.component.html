What is OCT?

Optical Coherence Tomography - The gold standard for retinal imaging

Non-Invasive Imaging

OCT uses light waves to capture detailed cross-sectional images of the retina. It's completely non-invasive and takes just seconds to perform, making it ideal for routine eye examinations.

  • High-resolution imaging
  • No radiation exposure
  • Quick and painless

Clinical Importance

Over 30 million OCT scans are performed annually worldwide. Early detection through OCT can prevent up to 95% of blindness cases in conditions like diabetic retinopathy and macular degeneration.

Diseases We Detect

Our AI model can accurately identify four major retinal conditions

CNV
Choroidal Neovascularization
Wet AMD indicator

Abnormal blood vessels grow from the choroid layer underneath the macula. These new vessels leak blood and fluid, causing damage to the photoreceptors and leading to rapid vision loss. This is the "wet" form of age-related macular degeneration.

Severity: High - Requires Immediate Attention
DME
Diabetic Macular Edema
Diabetes complication

A complication of diabetic retinopathy where fluid accumulates in the macula, causing it to swell and thicken. This leads to distorted vision and can cause permanent vision loss if untreated. Regular monitoring is crucial for diabetic patients.

Severity: Moderate - Requires Treatment
DRUSEN
Drusen (Early AMD)
Macular degeneration indicator

Yellow lipid deposits that form under the retinal pigment epithelium. Small drusen are common with aging, but large or numerous drusen indicate early age-related macular degeneration. Regular monitoring can help prevent progression to advanced AMD.

Severity: Low-Moderate - Monitoring Required
NORMAL
Normal Retina
Healthy eye

A healthy retina shows well-organized layers, a normal foveal contour, and no signs of fluid, deposits, or abnormal growths. Regular eye examinations are still recommended to maintain eye health and catch any early changes.

Status: Healthy - Continue Regular Checkups
